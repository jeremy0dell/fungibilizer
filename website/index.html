Why

NFTs don't split

You can't sell half an NFT, post one as collateral, or LP with it. Every existing workaround adds oracles, governance, or trust assumptions.

Floors are already fungible

Buyers bid on "any Punk" not a specific one. The market already treats floor NFTs as interchangeable — the token standard just hasn't caught up.

DeFi runs on ERC-20

AMMs, lending, options, yield — all built for fungible tokens. The Fungibilizer bridges the gap with a standard ERC-20 backed 1:1 by vaulted NFTs.

The Catalyst

Funging isn't free. It costs a Fungibilizer — scarce, transferable, burned on use.

Each Fungibilizer is an ERC-721 that lets you fung exactly one NFT. Then it's gone. This means there are two markets: one for the right to funge, and one for the pool tokens that come out the other side.

When lots of people want to funge, Fungibilizers get expensive. When nobody cares, they're worthless. The protocol doesn't set the price — the market does.

The market prices the act itself.

Use Cases

Instant liquidity

Can't find a buyer? Funge it, sell the pool tokens on any DEX. Done.

Floor index

Hold fPUNK to track the CryptoPunk floor without picking a specific one. It's an NFT ETF.

DeFi legos

Pool tokens are ERC-20. LP them, lend them, write options on them — whatever you'd do with any token.

Game mechanics

A one-shot power that dissolves a unique item into currency. Works for games, governance, social signaling.
